Diabetic Nephropathy Care

Protecting your kidney health when living with diabetes

Understanding Diabetic Kidney Disease

Diabetic nephropathy is a serious kidney-related complication of type 1 diabetes and type 2 diabetes. It affects about 40% of people with diabetes and can lead to kidney failure.

Early detection and proper management can slow or prevent the progression of kidney disease. Our nephrology team specializes in comprehensive care for diabetic kidney disease.

Key Tests Include:

Urine albumin, serum creatinine, GFR measurement, and blood pressure monitoring

Stages of Diabetic Nephropathy

Stage 1: Hyperfiltration

Kidneys are working harder than normal. There may be no symptoms, but the kidneys are enlarged.

Stage 2: Silent Stage

Kidneys begin to show damage with small amounts of protein leaking into urine (microalbuminuria).

Stage 3: Early Nephropathy

Protein in urine becomes detectable by routine tests (macroalbuminuria). Blood pressure may rise.

Stage 4: Advanced Nephropathy

Kidney function declines significantly. Waste products build up in blood. Anemia and bone disease may develop.

Stage 5: Kidney Failure

Kidneys lose nearly all ability to function. Dialysis or kidney transplant becomes necessary.

Prevention & Management

Blood Pressure Control

Maintaining blood pressure below 130/80 mmHg with ACE inhibitors or ARBs which also protect kidneys.

Blood Sugar Management

Keeping HbA1c below 7% through medication, diet, and lifestyle changes.

Dietary Changes

Reducing protein, salt, and phosphorus intake while maintaining proper nutrition.

Early Intervention Makes a Difference

With proper management, progression of diabetic kidney disease can often be slowed or prevented.
