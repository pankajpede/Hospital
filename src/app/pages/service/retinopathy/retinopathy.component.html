Diabetic Retinopathy Care

Protecting your vision from diabetes complications

Understanding Diabetic Retinopathy

Diabetic retinopathy is a diabetes complication that affects eyes. It's caused by damage to the blood vessels of the light-sensitive tissue at the back of the eye (retina).

At first, diabetic retinopathy may cause no symptoms or only mild vision problems. Eventually, it can cause blindness. The condition can develop in anyone who has type 1 or type 2 diabetes.

Regular eye exams are crucial for early detection and treatment of diabetic retinopathy.

Stages of Diabetic Retinopathy

Mild Nonproliferative

Small areas of balloon-like swelling in the retina's blood vessels.

Moderate Nonproliferative

Blood vessels that nourish the retina become blocked.

Severe Nonproliferative

More blood vessels become blocked, depriving areas of the retina.

Proliferative

New, abnormal blood vessels grow on the retina, which can lead to serious vision problems.

Treatment Options

Laser Treatment

Focal laser treatment can stop or slow the leakage of blood and fluid in the eye.

Injections

Medications called VEGF inhibitors can help reduce swelling and slow vision loss.

Vitrectomy

Surgical procedure to remove blood from the middle of the eye in advanced cases.

Annual Eye Exams Save Vision

If you have diabetes, yearly comprehensive dilated eye exams are essential even if your vision seems fine.
